01 Problem

Launching a satellite got cheap. Using it well did not.

A satellite earns nothing until it is pointed at the right target at the right moment and its data comes down through a ground station rented by the minute. Past about ten satellites, deciding that by hand stops working.

~$3 / minWhat a commercial ground-station minute costs. A wasted or failed pass is a line item, not an inconvenience.
Calendars, cron, spreadsheetsHow fleets are actually run today — in-house tools built in a hurry, priority rules in one engineer's head, 24/7 shifts.
~35%Improvement of optimised scheduling over greedy first-come-first-served in the published literature. That value is left on the table every week.
EvidencePer-minute pricing: Payload Space market floor (~$3/min), AWS Ground Station model. Workflow evidence: Yamcs #584, OpenC3 #1200/#647, SatNOGS community threads — the maintainers of both leading open-source control engines say planning is an external system. 35%: AIAA two-step binary-linear-programming paper vs greedy. India specifics: Pixxel and Dhruva job postings show in-house ground software and shift-based 24/7 operations centres.

03 Solution

One workflow, above the control system, denominated in money.

Constellation OS owns the operator's day from order to repair. The command-and-control system underneath keeps every safety authority it has today.

Requestorders arrive, typed and versioned
Plansolver chooses collections, downlinks, uplinks
Approvea human signs an exact version
Sendonly approved activities reach C2
Monitorreceipts show what really ran
Repairbounded re-solve when reality diverges
Money-firstThe objective is rupees: value delivered on time minus station minutes minus lateness. Every refused order carries a typed reason.
Proven, not guessedEach plan ships a solver-proven certificate that no plan exists worth more than 10% above it. That is what lets an operator approve fast.
Nothing to rip outIntegrates with the C2, flight-dynamics and ground-network tools the operator already built or bought. Self-hosted, on-prem, auditable.
BoundaryOnly a separate worker dispatches operator-approved, allow-listed activities to the C2 (Yamcs today). No browser, no AI model and no natural-language output can issue a spacecraft command or touch fault-protection settings. Flight software keeps that authority permanently. We never say "AI runs satellites", because it does not.

08 Competition

Three kinds of answer exist. None is domestic, on-prem and money-first.

New-space SaaS

Quindar, Cognitive Space, Leanspace, Saber. The category shape we are validating against — three years ahead, foreign cloud, no India presence, excluded from defence-adjacent work.

Legacy vendors

Kratos, GMV, Terma, L3Harris. Decade-to-field programme procurement. Not addressing a forty-person operator, and heuristic schedulers with no proven bound.

In-house

The real incumbent at Pixxel, Dhruva and GalaxEye. We do not replace it — we sit above it. Build-vs-buy flips as fleets pass ~10 satellites.

Relationships, not just rivalsDhruva is competitor, customer and channel at once — the most concentrated buyer of scheduling capability in India and a potential bundle with their Earth Stations. Digantara (SSA) and Epsilon3 (procedures) are partners. Antaris (Chennai/US) is the closest domestic name, positioned on satellite design and build rather than fleet-level economics. Funding figures from Tracxn and SpaceNews, mid-2026.

09 Go-to-market

A named-account market, entered through a design-partner programme.

1 · India, first doorKaleidEO (hybrid stack, actively building tasking — the softest entry), then GalaxEye and Pixxel (in-house incumbents, fleets doubling), then Dhruva as buyer and channel. 8–12 qualified accounts total.
2 · Sovereign programmesSBS-3's 31 private-built satellites arrive through integrators (Ananth, Centum, Alpha Design) who build the fleet but do not plan it. EO-PPP's ops decisions sit with Pixxel + Dhruva jointly. Slow, large, on-prem only.
3 · Small operators, globalThe segment Quindar-class pricing leaves behind: 5–30 satellite fleets in Europe, Australia, South-East Asia. Sold from India, self-hosted, priced per satellite.

The design-partner offer, one page

Three to five operators. Self-hosted install beside their C2, integration to their ground network, one 45-minute call a fortnight and async feedback within five days. Preferential pricing locked for two years. A hard end date with a binary ask: go paid or don't.

Bridging the 9–18-month cycle

Non-dilutive first: IN-SPACe Seed Fund (≤ ₹1 cr, milestone tranches), iDEX Prime Space (mission-planning is a named problem statement), Technology Adoption Fund (≤ ₹25 cr, up to 60% of cost). T-Hub's ORBIT SpaceTech accelerator is in the founder's own city.

Why this orderKaleidEO's public materials show a build-plus-buy model with mission planning under construction — the best design-partner hypothesis in the evidence base. Pixxel and Dhruva jointly control national-constellation ops decisions, so India is 2–3 concentrated doors rather than a volume market; concentrated-buyer risk is stated on the risks slide and countered by the global small-operator segment. Design-partner structure follows Bessemer and Tracsio guidance: small cohort, one-page agreement, defined commitments, conversion deadline.

Battle-tested components; the novelty is in how they are joined.

ComponentWhat it doesBuilt on
Orbital geometryTurns each satellite's element set into every physically possible imaging window, downlink and command-uplink pass. Deterministic, cached, stale inputs visible.TAT-C · Skyfield · SGP4 · OMM XML

Fixed safety boundary

Only the separate C2 worker may dispatch a typed, operator-approved, allow-listed activity. No browser or model gets arbitrary command access; no model output reaches commands, constraints or fault-protection configuration. Yamcs and flight software retain validation, queueing, uplink and FDIR authority.
